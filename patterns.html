Finding 04Even the agreed records aren’t the same object

Jazz and pop have their own version of the performance problem. The recording is fixed, but the release is not — remasterings, audiophile pressings, the occasional remix — and for this magazine the edition is frequently the whole point of the recommendation.

Kind of Blue, the most-agreed record in the corpus, is the test case. Five writers, six entries, four different editions over thirty years — and four of the six make the edition itself the argument.

YearWriterEdition citedWhat the entry argues
1992Jack W. EnglishColumbia/CBS 62066 — Dutch LP pressingargues for the releasePicks it for the Dutch pressings Chad Kassem had made available, and flags the current CD, CK 40579, as “not recommended in current version.”
1996Thomas ConradColumbia/Legacy Mastersound 24K gold CD, CK 52861argues for the release“Columbia’s production was a spectacular botch” — the tape ran slow and side one sat a quarter-tone sharp. A long-lost backup master fixed the speed on this edition.

2022Michael FremerAnalogue Productions UHQR AUHQR 0004argues for the release“There likely will still be no better-sounding, more detail-revealing version than this limited-to-25,000-copies UHQR release” — Grundman lacquers cut from the three-track masters.
2022Fred KaplanAnalogue Productions UHQR AUHQR 0004argues for the releaseHad cited it before, but the new reissue was “so superior to all previous pressings, and so musically revelatory,” that he “received dispensation from on high” to name it again.

The only true edition-level agreement here is Fremer and Kaplan, who both chose the 2021 UHQR in the same year. Note also Kaplan against himself: in 2007 he waved off pressing-chasing, and in 2022 a pressing was his stated reason for naming the album a second time.

Matching on catalogue number cannot always tell a genuinely new mastering from the same issue in another format, so this layer is read from the prose rather than counted.

Finding 13The comparative double-pick

Twice in the corpus a writer spent both slots on the same work in rival performances, and reviewed them against each other. In 1992 Robert Levine chose Bach’s B-minor Mass twice — Karl Richter’s reading against John Eliot Gardiner’s 1985 one, recorded 24 years apart. In 1996 Sam Tellig did the same with two Shostakovich Fifteenths, on Erato and Collins Classics.

Separately, ten writers came back to a record they had already chosen in an earlier year — fifteen cases in all. Two titles were chosen three times over by the same writer: Robert Levine’s Mozart La Clemenza di Tito in 2007, 2011 and 2020, and Allen St. John’s Kate and Anna McGarrigle in 1991, 1992 and 1994.

How this was counted

  • Source. All 36 articles, 1991–2026, parsed into 2,575 selections — the same data as the index.
  • Genre is inferred, not recorded. The articles carry no genre field, so each pick was classified from composer or artist, work title, performer credits and label. Hand-checking random samples puts accuracy near 90%; big bands, avant-garde vocal work, art song and film scores are the usual misses. Treat the shares as approximate and the trends as solid.
  • Recording identity. Popular titles are matched after stripping accents, articles and reissue notes. Classical titles name a work, so they are split by performance using performer credits and catalogue numbers — conservatively, so this under-counts agreement rather than inventing it.
  • Dates. Recording year is the earliest year stated in the catalog line, present for about 70% of picks.
  • Credits and labels are read from catalog lines, so anyone uncredited in the copy is invisible here.
